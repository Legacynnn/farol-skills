# Vision

Why this product exists, for whom, and what makes it worth choosing. Every spec's Problem section traces back here.

## One line

<Product> helps <who> <do what> so that <outcome>, unlike <alternative>, which <shortfall>.

## Users

One block per primary user type. Secondary types get one line each.

- **<Type>**: who they are, their context (device, frequency, pressure), the job they hire the product for, what they use today.

## Problems worth solving

Numbered, ordered by how much they cost the user today. Each one line.

1. <problem, from the user's perspective>

## Principles

Three to five sentences every product decision traces to. Written so two reasonable people could disagree with them.

1. <principle>

## Bets

What we believe that competitors do not, and what would prove us wrong.

- **<belief>**: falsified if <observable>.

## Not this

Explicit no-s: audiences, features, and channels we will not pursue, so nobody reopens them.

- <no>

## Measures

How we know it is working, three numbers at most, with today's value.

| Measure | Today | Target | Read from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Update triggers

- Audience, positioning or principles shift in a `grill-me` session → rewrite the affected section, date it
- A bet is falsified or confirmed → move it to Sources with the evidence
